# Losing 100 and finding 100

Psychology. About 60 seconds. Styles: warm-story, then study.

- In prospect theory a loss weighs more than a gain of the same size. Tversky and Kahneman, in their 1992 paper, report a typical coefficient of about 2.25: a loss a little more than twice the matching gain. That is a figure from their study, not a constant of every person. Say "about twice". Show 2.25 only with that caveat.
- The usual illustration is a coin flip that pays 150 or costs 100, which many people turn down. Tell it as an illustration of the shape of the preference. This note has no measured share of people, so the film does not invent one.

One coin, or one balance. The loss side dips further than the gain side rises. No faces.
