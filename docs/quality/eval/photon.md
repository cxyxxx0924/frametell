# A photon's way out of the Sun

Science. About 60 seconds. Styles: chalkboard, then warm-story.

A public film of this subject is [here](https://x.com/AstroTheWizard/status/2103629247751618782). This note is the material, not that film's prompt.

- The Sun's core is about 15 million °C.
- Light made there does not fly straight out. It is absorbed and sent on again so many times that published estimates of the time to the surface run from about 10,000 years to about 170,000 years. Give the range, or say "tens of thousands of years", and say that it depends on the model. Do not pick one year count and call it exact.
- From the surface to Earth is about 8 minutes 20 seconds: one astronomical unit at the speed of light.

One dot for the photon. It scatters its way out, then takes one straight hop to a small Earth. No face on it.
